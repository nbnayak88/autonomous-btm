### History and future editorial note

Organisations were changing long before 1970. “Picture 1970” scenes are illustrative teaching devices, not universal descriptions or a claim that transformation began then.

A historical architecture milestone is the development of TOGAF Version 1 in 1995, documented in MITRE's 2004 draft Guide to the Evolving Enterprise Architecture Body of Knowledge [1]. This is one part of the architecture story, not the origin of business transformation.

Present-day scenes are exercises; all 2050 scenes are imagined possibilities rather than forecasts. The definitions below are beginner-friendly teaching explanations, not verbatim framework definitions.

## Day 1 — Start with the Reason to Change

🎯 **DAY 1 OF 7 — THE TRANSFORMATION MINDSET SHIFT**

Welcome! Today, begin with a purpose rather than a product.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes: read, reflect and share.**

### 💭 “We need a new platform.”

Maybe. But what is the problem?

In our fictional learning service, learners receive conflicting instructions. A new platform might help—or it might distribute inconsistent information faster.

First ask: **What should become better, for whom, and why?**

An **output** is something delivered, such as a joining guide.

An **outcome** is a change in what happens, such as learners finding the correct session without avoidable confusion.

The guide is useful if it supports the outcome.

### 🕰️ A brief journey: 1970 → 2050

**Picture 1970:** A service changes its procedures, roles or information to solve a coordination problem. This is an illustrative scene.

**A historical milestone — 1995:** TOGAF Version 1 was developed as part of the enterprise architecture field [1]. Architecture frameworks are one strand in the wider transformation story.

**Today:** Connect strategy, people, processes, information and technology around a useful outcome.

**Imagine 2050:** AI could help explore options and monitor changes. People would still choose priorities, question evidence and remain accountable.

The future scene is a possibility, not a prediction.

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“What should we install?” → “What should become better?”**

### 🌍 People and impact

Confusing instructions can waste learners' time and create repeated work for volunteers. A useful change should consider both groups.

Ask who benefits and who carries the effort.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**Choosing not to add a tool can be a transformation decision.**

Clearer ownership and reliable information may be the first useful step.

## Day 2 — See the Business Behind the Service

🧩 **DAY 2 OF 7 — YOUR FIRST ARCHITECTURE LENS**

Yesterday, you identified a purpose. Today, look at what must work together.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. No architecture notation required.**

### 💭 Who makes a smooth learning session possible?

Someone confirms registrations. Someone prepares joining information. Someone grants appropriate access. Someone helps when things go wrong.

These activities connect.

A **capability** is something an organisation needs to be able to do, such as manage registrations or support learners.

A **process** describes how work happens.

A **value stream** follows the stages that create a useful result for a stakeholder, such as moving from registration to participation.

**Business architecture** helps make purpose, capabilities, value creation and responsibilities understandable.

**Enterprise architecture** connects the wider organisation, including business, information, applications and technology, to guide coherent change.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** Teams coordinate through people, procedures and records.

**Today:** The same service may depend on several applications and shared data.

**Imagine 2050:** AI helps reveal dependencies. People still check whether the map reflects real work.

The question: **“What must work together for this outcome?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Fix one department.” → “Understand the connections around the need.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

A change that saves one team time may create extra work elsewhere.

Include people who receive the handoff, not only those who start it.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**A capability is not an app.**

“Support learners” can involve a volunteer, a clear process and several tools. Renaming a system does not tell us whether the capability works.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Write three connected abilities your service needs.

- **Capability 2:** ___; responsible role ___.
- **Capability 3:** ___; responsible role ___.
- **One important connection or handoff:** ___

**Example:** Manage registration → prepare correct joining information → support participation.

Role names are enough. No real people need to be identified.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

Which capability most affects your Day 1 need? Keep that link. Your map should help a decision, not become a catalogue of everything.

## Day 3 — Describe Today and a Better Tomorrow

🔍 **DAY 3 OF 7 — CURRENT STATE, DESIRED STATE AND THE GAP**

You have a map. Today, make the change more specific.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. A few clear sentences are enough.**

### 💭 “The process is broken.”

Which part?

In the fictional service, one message shows an old session link while another shows a new one.

That is more useful than a broad complaint. It points to something to investigate.

The **current state** describes how things work now.

The **desired state** describes how you want them to work.

A **gap** is the difference that needs attention.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A team compares an existing procedure with a proposed one.

**Today:** Include roles, information, systems and the experience across the handoffs.

**Imagine 2050:** AI compares models and highlights differences. People still verify what is true and what is assumed.

The question: **“What exactly needs to change?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Everything needs replacing.” → “Which gap matters to the intended outcome?”**

### 🌍 People and impact

Different people may experience the same service differently.

A facilitator may see repeated questions; a learner may see unclear instructions. Both perspectives can improve your understanding.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**A desired state can be clear without being final.**

It is a direction to test and refine, not a promise that you have already solved every problem.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Reply:

- **My current-state observation:** ___
- **One assumption to verify:** ___
- **My desired state:** ___
- **The most important gap:** ___
- **One person or role whose perspective I need:** ___

**Example:** Fictional messages contain different links. I assume this causes confusion. Desired state: an agreed joining route with consistent instructions. Gap: no clear information owner. I would seek learner and facilitator perspectives.

You do not need to interview anyone. Use a fictional sample or describe a question you would ask.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

Does the gap connect to your Day 1 purpose and Day 2 capability? If your understanding changes, keep the earlier wording and record why you revised it.

## Day 4 — Compare Options Across the Whole System

⚖️ **DAY 4 OF 7 — THINK BEYOND THE TOOL**

You understand a gap. Today, compare two ways to address it.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. No software research required.**

### 💭 Two possible improvements

For our fictional service:

**Option A:** Agree one information owner, use a checked message template and maintain a simple session reference.

**Option B:** Use a shared joining page with an agreed owner, update process, access controls and a route to help.

Either needs people and processes. Technology alone does not establish who keeps information correct.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A team changes roles and procedures to improve coordination.

**Today:** Assess impacts across people, process, information and technology.

**Imagine 2050:** AI generates options and dependencies. People still judge feasibility and consequences.

The question: **“What else must change for this option to work?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Choose the most impressive solution.” → “Choose an option whose connections we understand.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

A joining page may help some learners while creating a barrier for those who cannot access it.

Consider access, support, staff effort and what information genuinely needs to be collected.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**The smallest visible change can have hidden dependencies.**

Updating one session link may affect messages, calendars, access and support instructions.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

**Example requirement:** “Joining instructions must show the currently approved session details and a support route.”

**Example check:** Compare a synthetic learner message with the approved fictional session record.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

## Day 5 — Sequence a Change People Can Adopt

🪜 **DAY 5 OF 7 — YOUR FIRST SMALL TRANSFORMATION ROADMAP**

You chose an option. Today, plan how to try it responsibly.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. Three steps are enough.**

### 💭 “Launch it to everyone tomorrow.”

What needs to happen first?

Someone must agree the content. Owners need to know their responsibilities. A small rehearsal may reveal a problem before wider use.

A **roadmap** shows a sequence of changes toward an intended outcome.

A **pilot** is a limited trial used to learn.

**Adoption** means people can and do use the changed way of working in the intended context.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A new procedure is explained and tried with one team.

**Today:** Sequence decisions, dependencies, preparation and feedback.

**Imagine 2050:** AI helps update a roadmap as evidence changes. People still agree priorities, responsibilities and limits.

The question: **“What must be true before we take the next step?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Announce the change.” → “Help people make the change work.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

A new process needs time and support. Quietly adding tasks to volunteers or staff can undermine the intended improvement.

Ask what they need to start—and what old activity can stop.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**A roadmap can include a decision not to expand.**

If the pilot exposes a serious problem, pausing can be a useful outcome.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Reply:

- **Step 1 — Prepare:** ___; owner ___.
- **Step 2 — Try on a small scale:** ___; owner ___.
- **Step 3 — Review before expanding:** ___; owner ___.
- **One dependency:** ___
- **One adoption/support action:** ___
- **Pause or review trigger:** “If ___, we will ___.”

**Example:** Agree the session record and owner → rehearse with a fictional learner message → review discrepancies before a small approved trial.

An owner can be a fictional role. No actual rollout is required.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

Which requirement does each step enable? Who has authority to approve a real change? Later, these links help connect a roadmap with governance and readiness.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have a three-step roadmap with ownership and a review point.

## Day 6 — Rehearse Before Go-Live

🧪 **DAY 6 OF 7 — CHECK THE CHANGE AND THE READINESS**

You have a roadmap. Today, test a small part of the proposed change.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. A mock message or rehearsal is enough.**

### 💭 The joining page works—until the session changes

A facilitator updates the session time. The page shows the new time, but the message template still shows the old one.

The page works technically. The service is inconsistent.

Testing should examine the connections that matter to the original need.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A team rehearses a procedure before wider use.

**Today:** Check the process, information, access, responsibilities and support—not only the screen.

**Imagine 2050:** AI generates more scenarios. People still check relevance, inspect results and decide what is ready.

The question: **“Does the changed way of working hold together?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“The feature works.” → “The intended service is checked and ready for its next step.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

Learners should not carry the burden of discovering basic inconsistencies. Staff also need a clear way to resolve issues.

Include a situation where something changes or an owner is unavailable.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**One honest failed rehearsal can prevent a larger failed rollout.**

A useful test is allowed to reveal that you are not ready.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Choose a Day 4 requirement. Create or inspect a tiny fictional sample.

Reply:

- **Requirement I am checking:** ___
- **Scenario:** ___
- **Expected result:** ___
- **What I checked or observed:** ___

A real go-live also needs an appropriate review of unresolved issues, data, access, support, communication, adoption and approvals. One passed sample is not complete readiness.

### 🧠 Mindset shift

### 🌍 People and impact

### ✨ A fresh angle

**A transformation can deliver everything on its task list and still miss the intended benefit.**

That is why the Origin Story stays connected throughout the work.

### 🧵 Meet the traceability matrix

A **traceability matrix** connects:

**Origin Story → need → capabilities and gaps → requirements → decisions → tests → readiness → outcomes.**

It helps us ask:

- During requirement gathering: “Which purpose and stakeholder need does this serve?”
- During testing: “Which requirement does this scenario verify?”
- Before go-live: “What is ready, what is unresolved and who owns the decision?”
- After use: “Was the change adopted, and what evidence suggests it helped?”

The links work in both directions. When a need changes, review the connected roadmap, requirements and checks.

## Coach-only appendix — Architecture, transformation and traceability

### Keep the introduction proportionate

The learner's output is a small transformation brief, not a complete enterprise transformation plan.

A local improvement can teach useful habits. Enterprise transformation normally involves a wider strategic scope and coordinated changes to capabilities, operating models and outcomes. Do not label every small process fix as a completed enterprise transformation.

Daily text submissions form starting evidence. Later bootcamps can introduce formal models, stable IDs, owners, versions, dependencies, benefit hypotheses, test evidence, defects and approvals.

The Origin Story gives purpose. It does not replace discovery, stakeholder validation, agreed requirements or governance.

### How the disciplines contribute

| Lens | Beginner question | Contribution to the shared case |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Why change, and what matters most? | Establish a useful participation outcome and constraints |
| Business architecture | What abilities, responsibilities and value-creating stages must connect? | Connect registration, joining information and learner support |
| Enterprise architecture | How do business, information, applications and technology fit together? | Connect owners, session records, communication tools and access |
| Process improvement | How does the work actually happen? | Examine how session details are created, updated and communicated |
| Operating model | Who decides, does, supports and reviews the work? | Assign ownership, update responsibilities and exception handling |
| Solution design | What implementable option meets agreed requirements? | Design a suitable page, message and update workflow |
| Portfolio and delivery | What should happen first, and what can we support? | Sequence preparation, rehearsal, pilot and review |
| Change and adoption | What helps people use the new way of working? | Prepare volunteers, explain changes and collect feedback |
| Governance and assurance | Who can approve, and what evidence is needed? | Review requirements, risks, readiness and unresolved issues |
| Value evaluation | What changed for stakeholders, and what remains uncertain? | Review participation experience, information consistency and staff effort |

These lenses overlap. They are teaching explanations, not a new mandatory framework or canonical list of BTM streams.

### Fictional case and scope

A community learning service has inconsistent joining instructions because registration, facilitators and support volunteers maintain separate session information.

The small exercise focuses on one session-information and joining flow. It does not redesign the entire business, automate membership entitlements or prescribe any actual SuccessLabs platform configuration.

**Proposed desired state:** Agreed session information has an accountable owner, reaches learners through a suitable route and has a support path when something changes.

**Potential capabilities:** Manage registrations; manage session information; support learner participation.

**Potential value stages:** Confirm participation → receive useful joining guidance → reach the session → obtain appropriate help.

Do not claim a business capability is an application or a value stream is identical to a department.

### Fictional traceability example

**OS-01 — Origin Story:** Learners should be able to find their correct session and materials without avoidable confusion.

**NEED-01 — Need to validate:** Intended learners receive understandable, consistent joining instructions and a suitable route to help.

All records below are proposed and unverified. No real implementation, test, adoption result, launch or benefit has occurred.

| Origin / need | Capability / gap | Requirement | Proposed decision | Acceptance scenario | Readiness / adoption | Outcome evidence after use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OS-01 / NEED-01 | CAP-01 Manage session information; GAP-01 no agreed information owner | REQ-01: Each session has an approved information record and accountable owner | DEC-01: Agree ownership, required fields and update responsibilities | TEST-01: Review a synthetic record for required fields, approval status and owner | READY-01: Owner and backup understand the process | OUT-01: Review discrepancies and how quickly ownership resolves them |
| OS-01 / NEED-01 | CAP-02 Communicate joining guidance; GAP-02 inconsistent messages | REQ-02: The learner-facing route shows approved current session details | DEC-02: Checked template or page, with an agreed update workflow | TEST-02: Change a fictional time and verify relevant sample messages/page reflect the approved record | READY-02: Volunteers know which version and route to use | OUT-02: Review whether learners could find and understand their next step |
| OS-01 / NEED-01 | CAP-03 Support participation; GAP-03 unclear help route | REQ-03: A suitable support path exists when instructions or access fail | DEC-03: Clear support contact, handoff and exception ownership | TEST-03: Rehearse an unavailable-primary-contact scenario with fictional roles | READY-03: Support capacity, backup and communication reviewed | OUT-03: Review unresolved queries and staff effort in context |
| OS-01 / NEED-01 | CAP-02 / CAP-03; GAP-04 access assumptions unverified | REQ-04: Intended learners can reach appropriate joining guidance without unnecessary disclosure | DEC-04: Agreed access and information-handling design | TEST-04: Positive and negative access scenarios with synthetic accounts; relevant accessibility review | READY-04: Responsible reviewers assess access, privacy and support | OUT-04: Review access difficulties using minimal necessary information |

A real design needs more detailed criteria and tests. The sample is not exhaustive and does not establish security or accessibility compliance.

### What traceability should reveal

- A feature with no clear connection to a stakeholder need.
- A requirement that does not address the agreed gap.
- A changed session record that has not flowed through connected messages.
- A requirement without a test, or a test without a relevant requirement.
- A pilot launched before ownership, support or adoption preparation.
- A technically working solution without evidence of the intended benefit.
- A change in purpose that has not prompted review of the roadmap.

Links can be many-to-many and must work in both directions. IDs are references, not proof. Keep actual evidence, status, owners and revision history.

An architecture model helps reveal relationships. A requirements matrix records coverage. A roadmap sequences work. A benefit review examines outcomes. They support each other and do not replace one another.

### Three decisions to keep distinct

| Decision | Evidence to consider | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Is the concept worth exploring? | Validated need, plausible options and constraints | That a solution is ready |
| Is the pilot or launch ready? | Relevant tests, ownership, support, unresolved issues and approvals | That the intended benefit has already occurred |
| Should the change continue or expand? | Actual use, feedback, outcomes, effort and new risks | That the result will generalise to every context |

A pause, revision or limited continuation can be a reasoned outcome. Do not equate approval with evidence that every assumption is true.

### Small value-evaluation plan

| Intended benefit | Possible evidence | Interpretation question |
|---|---|---|
| Learners find correct joining information | Task-based feedback and relevant support records | Who was able to respond, and who may be missing? |
| Information is consistent | Comparison of approved records and learner-facing samples | Did we check actual content, not only task completion? |
| Staff effort is sustainable | Volunteer feedback and repeated-work patterns | Was work removed, or shifted to another team? |
| People use the intended process | Appropriate observation and owner feedback | Do people understand it, and are workarounds telling us something useful? |

Agree baselines, definitions, review periods and owners before real evaluation. Do not invent targets or attribute a benefit to one change without considering other influences.

### Connection to the other Origin Story challenges

The shared pattern is:

**Purpose → need → requirements → decision → checks → readiness → outcome.**

Procurement, HR, CX, Finance and SCM each explore a domain-specific thread. BTM connects changes across those domains through purpose, architecture, delivery, adoption and value.

Leadership & Identity Transformation will explore how a person chooses and practises the behaviours needed to lead such change.

### Facilitation and tool notes

- Stay capability-first and platform-neutral.
- Do not require TOGAF terminology, modelling software or a formal notation to complete the challenge.
- Later architecture, process and delivery demonstrations can use appropriate tools after needs and governance are clear.
- Ask for a decision-useful map, not a complete inventory of the enterprise.
- On Day 3, distinguish observation from assumption.
- On Day 4, ask what else the proposed option affects.
- On Day 5, ask who owns adoption and what permits expansion.
- On Day 6, accept “Not tested yet” with a useful plan.
- On Day 7, distinguish outputs, adoption and outcomes.
- No promise of employment, certification, guaranteed benefits or automatic programme inclusion.
